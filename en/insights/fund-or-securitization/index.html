Fund or securitization? How to choose the vehicle.

FIAGRO and FII or CRA and CRI? The choice of structure defines cost, tenor, governance and the type of investor you'll reach.

Every capital markets transaction begins with a deceptively simple question: what is the best vehicle? In agribusiness and real estate, the decision usually comes down to two families of instruments — investment funds (FIAGRO and FII) and securitization notes (CRA and CRI). Both access the same market, but they solve different problems.

Two distinct logics

A fund is a collective investment vehicle: it pools resources to build and manage a portfolio of assets over time. There is active management, an investment policy and often a longer horizon. It is the natural instrument when the goal is to build and manage a portfolio — properties, land, equity or receivables.

Securitization, on the other hand, is a funding operation: it turns a specific set of receivables into a tradable note with a defined cash flow. The CRA and the CRI exist to advance receivables and finance a concrete transaction, with a relatively predictable beginning, middle and end.

A fund is for building and managing a portfolio. Securitization is for financing a specific receivables flow.

When a fund makes more sense

  • You want to build and manage a portfolio of assets over time, not just finance one transaction.
  • There is interest in fund governance, with administrator, manager and potential exchange listing.
  • The goal includes raising capital repeatedly or providing liquidity to holders via a secondary market.
  • In agri, a FIAGRO can combine rural real estate, receivables and equity in a single vehicle; in real estate, an FII brings together equity, credit or development.

When securitization is the way

  • There is an identifiable set of receivables (lease contracts, CPRs, financings) ready to back the issuance.
  • The goal is to raise funds now, advancing future flows at a competitive cost.
  • You are looking for broad investor demand — and the individual tax exemption of the CRA and CRI is a strong draw.
  • The transaction has a defined tenor and flow, favoring a structure with scheduled amortization.

It isn't a binary choice

In practice, funds and securitization coexist — and often complement each other. A credit-rights FIAGRO can hold CRAs in its portfolio; a credit FII is essentially a portfolio of CRIs. The right question is rarely "fund or securitization," but which combination best balances cost, tenor, investor base and governance for your goal.
